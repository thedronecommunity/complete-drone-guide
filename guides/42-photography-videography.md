## Aerial Photography Fundamentals

Drone photography gives perspectives impossible from the ground. The key challenge is getting sharp, well-exposed images from a moving, vibrating platform in the sky.

    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │         AERIAL SHOT TYPES                       │
    │                                                  │
    │  Bird's Eye:   Camera straight down (90°)       │
    │  Oblique:      Camera at 30-60° angle           │
    │  Reveal:       Fly forward, object appears       │
    │  Orbit:        Circle around subject             │
    │  Flyover:      Straight pass over subject        │
    │  Top-down:     Straight down, moving forward     │
    │  Dronie:       Fly backward + up from subject    │
    │  Helix:        Orbit while ascending             │
    └──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

## Camera Settings for Aerial

### Photography

    Mode: Manual or Aperture Priority
    ├── ISO: 100-400 (lowest possible)
    ├── Aperture: f/4-f/8 (sharpest range)
    ├── Shutter: 1/500s minimum (freeze motion)
    ├── Format: RAW (for post-processing flexibility)
    ├── Focus: Manual, set to infinity or hyperfocal
    ├── White balance: Daylight or Auto (correct in post)
    └── Metering: Evaluative/Matrix

### Videography

    Mode: Manual
    ├── Resolution: 4K (3840×2160) minimum
    ├── Frame rate: 24fps (cinematic), 30fps (standard), 60fps (slow-mo)
    ├── Shutter: 1/(2× frame rate) — 180° rule
    │   24fps → 1/50s, 30fps → 1/60s, 60fps → 1/120s
    ├── ND filter: REQUIRED in daylight to maintain shutter rule
    ├── ISO: Lowest possible
    ├── Color profile: Flat/Log (D-Cinelike, S-Log) for grading
    └── Bitrate: Highest available

### ND Filter Guide

| Condition | ND Filter | Stops Reduced |
|-----------|----------|--------------|
| Overcast | ND4 | 2 stops |
| Partly cloudy | ND8 | 3 stops |
| Sunny | ND16 | 4 stops |
| Bright sun, snow/water | ND32 | 5 stops |
| Extremely bright | ND64 | 6 stops |

## Gimbal and Stabilization

    For smooth video:
    ├── 3-axis gimbal (roll, pitch, yaw stabilization)
    ├── Vibration dampening between frame and gimbal
    ├── EIS (Electronic Image Stabilization) as backup
    └── Smooth stick inputs (expo on RC sticks)

    Without gimbal (GoPro on FPV):
    ├── Use GoPro HyperSmooth (EIS)
    ├── TPU soft mount (absorbs vibration)
    ├── Fly smoothly (no jerky movements)
    └── Post-stabilization: ReelSteady, Gyroflow

### Gyroflow (Free Stabilization)

    Gyroflow uses FC gyro data to stabilize footage:
    1. Record blackbox log during flight
    2. Record video on GoPro simultaneously
    3. Import both into Gyroflow
    4. Software syncs gyro data to video
    5. Renders stabilized output

    Result: Gimbal-like smoothness from a soft-mounted GoPro

## Composition for Aerial Shots

    Rules:
    ├── Rule of thirds (place subject at intersection)
    ├── Leading lines (roads, rivers, fences draw the eye)
    ├── Shadows create depth (shoot during golden hour)
    ├── Patterns and textures (fields, buildings from above)
    ├── Scale reference (include people, cars for scale)
    └── Negative space (empty sky or water adds mood)

    Best times:
    ├── Golden hour (sunrise/sunset): Warm light, long shadows
    ├── Blue hour (just before sunrise/after sunset): Moody
    ├── Overcast: Even light, no harsh shadows (good for mapping)
    └── Midday: Worst for photography (flat, harsh), OK for mapping
