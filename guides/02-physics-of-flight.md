## Four Forces of Flight

Every aircraft balances four fundamental forces:

```
                    LIFT
                     ↑
                     |
        DRAG ←───────┼───────→ THRUST
                     |
                     ↓
                   WEIGHT
```

### 1. Lift (Upward)

The force that opposes gravity and keeps the aircraft airborne.

| Aircraft Type | How Lift Is Generated |
|--------------|----------------------|
| Fixed-wing | Wings create pressure differences |
| Helicopter | Rotating blades |
| Quadcopter | Propellers push air down |
| Rocket | Pure thrust (no aerodynamic lift) |

### 2. Weight (Downward)

Total mass including:
- Airframe structure
- Propulsion system (motors, ESCs)
- Payload (camera, sensors)
- Fuel/battery
- Electronics

**Key insight**: Every gram matters. Lighter = longer flight time.

### 3. Thrust (Forward/Upward)

Force that propels the aircraft, generated by:
- Propellers
- Jet engines
- Rockets
- Ducted fans

### 4. Drag (Resistance)

Forces opposing motion through air:

| Drag Type | Cause | Reduction |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| **Parasitic** | Shape resistance | Streamlined design |
| **Induced** | Cost of creating lift | Efficient wings/props |
| **Interference** | Component interactions | Clean mounting |
| **Wave** | Supersonic speeds | Not relevant for drones |

## Quadcopter Movement

Unlike planes or helicopters, quadcopters control movement by changing individual motor speeds. There are no control surfaces.

### Basic Movements

| Movement | Motor Adjustment |
|----------|-----------------|
| **Rise** | All motors speed up equally |
| **Descend** | All motors slow down equally |
| **Forward** | Front motors slow, rear speed up |
| **Backward** | Front motors speed up, rear slow |
| **Right** | Left motors speed up, right slow |
| **Left** | Right motors speed up, left slow |
| **Rotate CW** | Motors 1&4 (CCW pair) speed up, 2&3 (CW pair) slow |
| **Rotate CCW** | Motors 2&3 (CW pair) speed up, 1&4 (CCW pair) slow |

### Why This Works

```
         FRONT
    Motor 4     Motor 2
    (CCW)       (CW)
         \     /
          \   /
           \ /
            X
           / \
          /   \
         /     \
    Motor 3     Motor 1
    (CW)        (CCW)
         BACK
```

**Critical Motor Rotation Pattern:**
- Front-right (2) & rear-left (3): **Clockwise (CW)**
- Front-left (4) & rear-right (1): **Counter-clockwise (CCW)**

This alternating pattern cancels torque. Without it, the drone would spin uncontrollably.

**Why pitch/roll and yaw use different motor pairings:**

Pitch and roll mixing groups motors by *physical position* (front vs. rear, left vs. right), because tilting the thrust vector is what moves the drone horizontally. Yaw mixing groups motors by *spin direction* instead (the two diagonal motors that spin the same way), because rotation comes from reaction torque, not tilt.

Each spinning propeller pushes back against the frame in the direction opposite its own spin (Newton's third law). Speeding up the CW pair (2 & 3) increases the CCW reaction torque on the frame, yawing it CCW. Speeding up the CCW pair (1 & 4) increases the CW reaction torque, yawing it CW. Because 1 & 4 and 2 & 3 are each split evenly between one front and one rear motor, total front thrust and total rear thrust stay balanced — so the drone yaws in place instead of also pitching.

A common mistake is pairing 1&3 or 2&4 for yaw — but those are the front pair and rear pair, not the same-spin-direction pair, so that mixing produces a pitch (forward/backward tilt) instead of a clean rotation.

### Movement Physics

**Forward flight example:**
1. Rear motors speed up
2. Rear of drone lifts
3. Drone tilts forward
4. Lift vector now has horizontal component
5. Drone moves forward

**Rotate CW example:**
1. Motors 1 & 4 (the CCW pair) speed up; motors 2 & 3 (the CW pair) slow down
2. Total front thrust and total rear thrust stay roughly equal, so the drone doesn't pitch
3. The CCW pair's reaction torque now dominates, pushing the frame CW
4. Drone rotates in place, nose swinging clockwise (viewed from above)

The flight controller does this automatically - you just push the stick (or twist the yaw control).

## Bernoulli's Principle

**Core Concept:** Faster airflow = lower pressure | Slower airflow = higher pressure

### How Wings Create Lift

```
        ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~>  (Fast air, LOW pressure)
       /                    \
      /                      \
     /________________________\
     ─────────────────────────>  (Slow air, HIGH pressure)
```

1. Curved top surface → air travels faster → lower pressure
2. Flat bottom surface → air travels slower → higher pressure
3. Pressure difference → net upward force (lift)

### Lift Factors

| Factor | Effect | Example |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Wing area | 2x area = 2x lift | Larger props = more lift |
| Air speed | 2x speed = 4x lift | Faster props = much more lift |
| Air density | Thicker air = more lift | Harder to fly at altitude |
| Angle of attack | More angle = more lift (to a point) | Tilting propeller blade |

## Angle of Attack (AoA)

The angle between the wing/blade and the oncoming air.

### AoA Ranges

| Range | Description | Characteristics |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| 0-5° | Low AoA | Smooth flow, moderate lift, minimal drag |
| 5-10° | Moderate AoA | Best lift-to-drag ratio, efficient cruise |
| 10-15° | High AoA | Increasing lift, rapidly increasing drag |
| 15-18° | Critical AoA | Maximum lift before stall |
| >18° | Stall | Airflow separates, lift drops 50%+ |

### Stall

When AoA exceeds the critical angle, airflow separates from the wing surface and lift collapses.

**Warning Signs:**
- Buffeting/shaking
- Mushy controls
- Warning horn (if equipped)
- Nose-high attitude

**Recovery:**
1. Push stick forward (reduce AoA)
2. Add full power
3. Level wings
4. Gradually pull out

**For quadcopters:** Stall is less of a concern because propellers operate at constant optimal angles. However, descending too fast can cause "vortex ring state" - similar effect.

## Control Axes

Aircraft rotate around three axes:

```
                    YAW (Vertical)
                        ↑
                        |
                    ────┼────
                   /    |    \
         ROLL ←──/─────┼─────\──→ ROLL
        (Bank)  /      |      \  (Bank)
               /       |       \
              /        ↓        \
                     PITCH
                   (Nose up/down)
```

| Axis | Movement | Controlled By (Fixed-Wing) | Controlled By (Quad) |
|------|----------|---------------------------|---------------------|
| **Pitch** | Nose up/down | Elevator | Front/rear motor differential |
| **Roll** | Bank left/right | Ailerons | Left/right motor differential |
| **Yaw** | Rotate left/right | Rudder | CW/CCW motor differential |

---

## Control Surfaces (Fixed-Wing)

### Primary Surfaces

| Surface | Location | Function | How It Works |
|---------|----------|----------|--------------|
| **Ailerons** | Wing trailing edges | Roll control | Work in opposition (one up, one down) |
| **Elevator** | Horizontal tail | Pitch control | Up elevator = tail down = nose up |
| **Rudder** | Vertical tail | Yaw control | Coordinated with ailerons in turns |

### Secondary Surfaces

| Surface | Purpose | Typical Range |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| **Flaps** | Increase lift/drag for takeoff/landing | 0-40° |
| **Spoilers** | Disrupt lift for rapid descent | As needed |

## Helicopter Mechanics

Understanding helicopters helps understand why quadcopters are simpler.

### Main Rotor

- 2-7 blades spinning at constant RPM (200-500)
- Control via blade pitch changes, NOT speed
- Each blade changes pitch as it rotates (cyclic)
- Complex mechanical systems required

### Tail Rotor

**Problem:** Main rotor torque would spin the fuselage opposite direction.

**Solutions:**
- Tail rotor (most common)
- Tandem rotors (Chinook)
- Coaxial rotors (counter-rotating stacked rotors)
- NOTAR (jet exhaust)

### Helicopter Controls

| Control | What It Does | Quadcopter Equivalent |
|---------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Collective** | All blades pitch together → up/down | Throttle (all motors) |
| **Cyclic** | Blades pitch varies through rotation → tilt | Pitch/roll stick |
| **Pedals** | Tail rotor thrust → yaw | Yaw stick |

### Why Quadcopters Are Simpler

| Aspect | Helicopter | Quadcopter |
|--------|------------|------------|
| Moving parts | Swashplate, pitch links, tail drive | Just motors |
| Failure modes | Many mechanical | Few |
| Maintenance | High | Low |
| Cost | High | Low |
| Efficiency | Higher | Lower |
| Complexity | Very high | Moderate |

## Special Flight Conditions

### Ground Effect

Within one rotor diameter of ground:
- Air compressed beneath creates extra lift
- Requires less power to hover
- Can make landing tricky (drone "floats")

### Translational Lift

When moving forward at 16-24 mph:
- Fresh air enters rotor disc
- More efficient than hover
- Transition can feel wobbly

### Vortex Ring State (Descending)

When descending too fast:
- Drone descends into its own downwash
- Vortices form at blade tips
- Loss of lift despite motor power
- **Recovery:** Move forward to escape own downwash

## Practical Applications

### Prop Selection Physics

| Want More... | Choose... | Trade-off |
|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| Thrust | Larger diameter | More current, slower response |
| Speed | Higher pitch | Less hover efficiency |
| Efficiency | Lower pitch, larger diameter | Less top speed |
| Response | Smaller, lighter props | Less thrust |

### Weight Matters

```
Flight time ∝ Battery capacity / (Weight × Power per kg)
```

Every 100g added:
- Reduces flight time by ~5-10%
- Requires more thrust
- Increases current draw
- Heats motors/ESCs more

### Altitude Effects

| Altitude | Air Density | Effect |
|----------|-------------|--------|
| Sea level | 100% | Normal |
| 1000m | ~90% | Slightly harder |
| 2000m | ~80% | Noticeably harder |
| 3000m | ~70% | Significantly harder |

Motors must spin faster to generate same lift in thin air.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Four forces**: Lift, weight, thrust, drag - all must balance
2. **Quadcopter control**: All movement from motor speed changes
3. **Motor pattern**: Alternating CW/CCW cancels torque
4. **Pitch/roll** mix by physical position (front/rear, left/right); **yaw** mixes by spin direction (diagonal same-spin pair), not position
5. **Bernoulli**: Fast air = low pressure = lift
6. **Three axes**: Pitch, roll, yaw
7. **Weight matters**: Every gram affects flight time
